# Clinical Practice Guidelines: Cardiovascular Disease and Hypertension

**Source:** American College of Cardiology (ACC) / American Heart Association (AHA) Guidelines  
**Category:** Cardiology / Vascular Medicine  
**Target Audience:** Healthcare Providers and Patients  

---

## 1. Blood Pressure Classification (ACC/AHA 2017/2023)

Blood pressure (BP) is categorized for adult populations as:

1. **Normal BP:** Systolic < 120 mmHg AND Diastolic < 80 mmHg.
2. **Elevated BP:** Systolic 120 - 129 mmHg AND Diastolic < 80 mmHg.
3. **Stage 1 Hypertension:** Systolic 130 - 139 mmHg OR Diastolic 80 - 89 mmHg.
4. **Stage 2 Hypertension:** Systolic >= 140 mmHg OR Diastolic >= 90 mmHg.
5. **Hypertensive Crisis:** Systolic > 180 mmHg and/or Diastolic > 120 mmHg.

### Hypertension Treatment Thresholds
- **Elevated BP:** Non-pharmacological lifestyle therapy (DASH diet, sodium reduction < 1500 mg/day, regular physical activity, alcohol limitation, weight loss).
- **Stage 1 Hypertension:** Lifestyle therapy alone if 10-year ASCVD risk < 10%; lifestyle PLUS blood pressure lowering medication if 10-year ASCVD risk >= 10% or if patient has diabetes or chronic kidney disease (CKD).
- **Stage 2 Hypertension:** Lifestyle therapy PLUS 2 first-line antihypertensive agents of different classes.
- **First-Line Antihypertensive Classes:** Thiazide diuretics (Chlorthalidone, Hydrochlorothiazide), CCBs (Amlodipine), ACE inhibitors (Lisinopril), or ARBs (Losartan). *Never combine ACE inhibitors and ARBs simultaneously.*

## 2. Coronary Artery Disease & Acute Coronary Syndrome (ACS)

### Clinical Presentation of Chest Pain
- **Typical Angina:** Substernal chest discomfort with characteristic pressure, squeezing, or heaviness; precipitated by physical exertion or emotional stress; relieved within minutes by rest or sublingual nitroglycerin.
- **Atypical Angina:** Discomfort that meets 2 of the 3 typical criteria. More common in women, elderly patients, and patients with diabetes (may present primarily as shortness of breath, fatigue, nausea, or epigastric discomfort).
- **Acute Myocardial Infarction (Red Flag Emergency):** Severe crushing retrosternal pain lasting > 20 minutes, radiation to left arm, neck, jaw, or epigastrium, accompanied by diaphoresis, dyspnea, nausea, and lightheadedness.

### Emergency Response Protocol for Suspected ACS
1. **Immediate Call to Emergency Medical Services (108 / 911):** Do not allow the patient to drive.
2. **Aspirin:** Administer 162 mg to 325 mg non-enteric-coated aspirin to chew immediately (unless severe allergy or active GI bleeding).
3. **Rest & Vital Signs:** Keep patient seated and calm, administer supplemental oxygen only if SpO2 < 90%.
4. **Nitroglycerin:** 0.4 mg sublingually every 5 minutes (up to 3 doses) if systolic BP > 90 mmHg and no PDE-5 inhibitor use within past 24-48 hours.

## 3. Lipid Management & Atherosclerotic Risk Reduction

### Lipid Panel Targets
- **Total Cholesterol:** Desirable < 200 mg/dL.
- **LDL-C (Bad Cholesterol):** Optimal < 100 mg/dL (< 70 mg/dL or < 55 mg/dL for very high ASCVD risk).
- **HDL-C (Good Cholesterol):** Protective >= 50 mg/dL in females, >= 40 mg/dL in males.
- **Triglycerides:** Normal < 150 mg/dL.

### Statin Therapy Indications
- **Secondary Prevention:** High-intensity statin (Atorvastatin 40-80 mg or Rosuvastatin 20-40 mg) for all patients with clinical ASCVD.
- **Primary Prevention:**
  - Severe Hypercholesterolemia (LDL >= 190 mg/dL): High-intensity statin without needing ASCVD risk calculation.
  - Diabetes aged 40-75 with LDL 70-189 mg/dL: Moderate-intensity statin (e.g., Atorvastatin 20 mg).
  - High 10-year ASCVD risk (>= 20%): High-intensity statin.
