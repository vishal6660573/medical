# Clinical Reference: Pharmacotherapy Safety, Interactions, and Contraindications

**Source:** WHO Essential Medicines Guidelines & FDA Drug Safety Communications  
**Category:** Clinical Pharmacology / Medication Safety  
**Target Audience:** Healthcare Professionals and Clinical Support Systems  

---

## 1. High-Risk Drug Interactions and Contraindications

### Cardiovascular & Renal Medications
- **ACE Inhibitors (e.g., Lisinopril) + ARBs (e.g., Losartan):** Dual RAS blockade is contraindicated due to heightened risk of severe hyperkalemia, acute kidney injury (AKI), and hypotension without additional clinical benefit.
- **ACE Inhibitors / ARBs + Potassium-Sparing Diuretics / Supplements (e.g., Spironolactone):** Requires baseline and regular serum potassium and creatinine monitoring to prevent lethal hyperkalemia.
- **Non-Steroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drugs (NSAIDs like Ibuprofen, Naproxen, Diclofenac):**
  - **Triple Whammy Effect:** Combination of an ACEi/ARB + Diuretic + NSAID precipitously reduces glomerular filtration pressure, causing acute renal failure.
  - **Hypertension & Heart Failure:** NSAIDs promote sodium and fluid retention and attenuate the efficacy of antihypertensives.
  - **Peptic Ulcer Disease:** Avoid non-selective NSAIDs or co-prescribe a proton pump inhibitor (PPI).

### Antidiabetic Agents
- **Metformin:** Contraindicated in severe renal impairment (eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m2) due to risk of lactic acidosis. Withhold 48 hours prior to iodinated radiocontrast procedures in patients with eGFR 30-60 mL/min.
- **Sulfonylureas (e.g., Glipizide, Glimepiride):** High risk of prolonged hypoglycemia, especially in elderly patients and those with renal impairment. Prefer short-acting agents or DPP-4 inhibitors.
- **SGLT2 Inhibitors (e.g., Empagliflozin):** Risk of euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis (euDKA) and mycotic genital infections. Discontinue 3-4 days prior to elective surgery.

## 2. Drug Allergy Documentation and Cross-Reactivity
- **True IgE-Mediated Allergy vs Adverse Effect:** Distinguish urticaria, bronchospasm, facial/laryngeal angioedema, and anaphylaxis from benign side effects like nausea or ACE-inhibitor cough.
- **Penicillin - Cephalosporin Cross-Reactivity:** Cross-reactivity in patients with proven penicillin allergy is < 2% with 3rd/4th generation cephalosporins (e.g., Ceftriaxone, Cefepime). However, if there is a history of severe anaphylaxis or Steven-Johnson syndrome, avoid all beta-lactams unless formal desensitization is conducted.
- **Sulfa Allergies:** Distinguish sulfonamide antibiotics (Sulfamethoxazole) from non-antibiotic sulfonamides (Furosemide, Celecoxib) which have negligible cross-reactivity.

---

## 3. Principles of Dosage Adjustment
- **Renal Impairment:** Calculate estimated creatinine clearance (CrCl via Cockcroft-Gault) or eGFR prior to prescribing renally cleared antimicrobials, anticoagulants (DOACs like Apixaban), and antidiabetics.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Exercise extreme caution with hepatically metabolized drugs with narrow therapeutic windows (e.g., Acetaminophen max dose reduced to <= 2 g/day in chronic liver disease; avoid sedatives that precipitate hepatic encephalopathy).
