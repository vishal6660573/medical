# Clinical Practice Guidelines: Respiratory Pathologies and Radiographic Assessment

**Source:** American Thoracic Society (ATS) / Infectious Diseases Society of America (IDSA)  
**Category:** Pulmonology / Infectious Disease / Radiology  
**Target Audience:** Healthcare Providers and Patients  

---

## 1. Community-Acquired Pneumonia (CAP)

### Clinical Presentation
Pneumonia is an infection of the pulmonary parenchyma presenting with:
- **Core Symptoms:** Cough (productive with purulent or rust-colored sputum, or dry), fever, chills, rigors, pleuritic chest pain, dyspnea, fatigue.
- **Physical Examination Signs:** Tachypnea, crackles/rales on auscultation, localized bronchial breath sounds, dullness to percussion, increased tactile fremitus.
- **Elderly / Immunocompromised Presentation:** May present atypically with confusion, altered mental status, hypothermia, or falls without prominent fever or cough.

### Diagnostic Criteria
- Demonstration of a new infiltrate/consolidation on Chest Radiograph (CXR) or CT scan in the presence of clinically compatible acute respiratory symptoms.

---

## 2. Chest Radiograph (X-Ray) Pattern Interpretation

### Common Radiographic Patterns
1. **Normal Chest Radiograph:**
   - Clear bilateral lung fields without focal opacities, consolidations, or pleural effusions.
   - Sharp costophrenic angles and normal cardiothoracic ratio (< 0.50 on PA view).
2. **Bacterial Lobar Pneumonia:**
   - Homogeneous focal alveolar consolidation with air bronchograms, typically confined to an anatomical lobe or segment (e.g., Streptococcus pneumoniae, Klebsiella).
3. **Viral & Atypical Pneumonia / COVID-19:**
   - Bilateral, multifocal, peripheral, and basal ground-glass opacities (GGO) or interstitial reticular markings.
   - Absence of discrete cavitation, mediastinal adenopathy, or large pleural effusions in early stages.

## 3. Severity Stratification & CURB-65 Score

CURB-65 is a validated clinical score to determine the setting of care (outpatient vs inpatient vs ICU) for adult pneumonia:
- **C - Confusion:** Mental status impairment (Abbreviated Mental Test score <= 8 or new disorientation).
- **U - Urea:** Blood Urea Nitrogen > 19 mg/dL (7 mmol/L).
- **R - Respiratory Rate:** >= 30 breaths/min.
- **B - Blood Pressure:** Systolic < 90 mmHg OR Diastolic <= 60 mmHg.
- **65 - Age:** >= 65 years.

### Setting Recommendation
- **Score 0 - 1:** Low risk (mortality < 3%). Suitable for outpatient home management with oral antibiotics.
- **Score 2:** Moderate risk (mortality ~9%). Short inpatient stay or closely supervised outpatient management.
- **Score 3 - 5:** High risk (mortality 15-40%). Inpatient hospital admission; score 4-5 warrants immediate ICU evaluation.

---

## 4. Red Flag Respiratory Emergencies
Patients exhibiting any of the following require urgent emergency department transfer:
- Oxygen saturation (SpO2) < 92% on room air (or < 88% in chronic hypercapnic COPD).
- Severe respiratory distress with accessory muscle use, stridor, or inability to speak full sentences.
- Hemoptysis (coughing up blood).
- Cyanosis of lips, nail beds, or tongue.
- Systolic BP < 90 mmHg with signs of systemic hypoperfusion/sepsis.
