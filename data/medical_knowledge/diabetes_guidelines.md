# Clinical Practice Guidelines: Diabetes Mellitus Diagnosis and Management

**Source:** American Diabetes Association (ADA) Standards of Medical Care & WHO Guidelines  
**Category:** Endocrinology / Metabolic Health  
**Target Audience:** Healthcare Providers and Patients  

---

## 1. Diagnostic Criteria for Diabetes Mellitus

Diabetes can be diagnosed using any of the following four criteria (confirmed by repeat testing on a separate day unless unequivocal symptoms of hyperglycemia are present):

1. **Fasting Plasma Glucose (FPG):** >= 126 mg/dL (7.0 mmol/L). Fasting is defined as no caloric intake for at least 8 hours.
2. **2-Hour Post-Load Plasma Glucose (2-h PG):** >= 200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L) during an Oral Glucose Tolerance Test (OGTT) using a 75 g anhydrous glucose load.
3. **Hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c):** >= 6.5% (48 mmol/mol) performed in a certified laboratory using an NGSP-certified assay.
4. **Random Plasma Glucose:** >= 200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L) in a patient with classic symptoms of hyperglycemia (polyuria, polydipsia, polyphagia, unexplained weight loss) or hyperglycemic crisis.

### Prediabetes Categories (Increased Risk of Diabetes)
- **Impaired Fasting Glucose (IFG):** FPG 100 mg/dL to 125 mg/dL (5.6 - 6.9 mmol/L).
- **Impaired Glucose Tolerance (IGT):** 2-h PG 140 mg/dL to 199 mg/dL (7.8 - 11.0 mmol/L).
- **Prediabetes A1c:** 5.7% to 6.4% (39 - 47 mmol/mol).

## 2. Glycemic Goals and Monitoring

For non-pregnant adults with diabetes:
- **A1c Target:** < 7.0% (53 mmol/mol) for most non-pregnant adults. Less stringent targets (< 8.0%) are appropriate for individuals with extensive comorbid conditions, limited life expectancy, or high risk of severe hypoglycemia. More stringent targets (< 6.5%) may be considered for newly diagnosed patients without significant cardiovascular disease.
- **Preprandial Capillary Blood Glucose:** 80 - 130 mg/dL (4.4 - 7.2 mmol/L).
- **Peak Postprandial Capillary Blood Glucose (1-2 hours after meal):** < 180 mg/dL (< 10.0 mmol/L).

## 3. Pharmacological Management of Type 2 Diabetes

### First-Line Therapy
- **Metformin** is the preferred initial pharmacologic agent for the treatment of type 2 diabetes in the absence of contraindications (e.g., eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m2, severe hepatic impairment, acute metabolic acidosis).
- Starting dose: 500 mg once or twice daily with meals, titrated gradually to 2000 mg/day in divided doses to minimize gastrointestinal adverse effects.
- Comprehensive lifestyle modifications (medical nutrition therapy, >= 150 minutes/week of moderate-intensity aerobic exercise, and weight management) must accompany pharmacotherapy.

### Second-Line and Add-on Agents
- **Patients with Atherosclerotic Cardiovascular Disease (ASCVD) or High CV Risk:** SGLT2 inhibitors (e.g., Empagliflozin, Dapagliflozin) or GLP-1 receptor agonists (e.g., Semaglutide, Liraglutide) with proven CVD benefit are recommended independent of baseline A1c.
- **Patients with Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) or Heart Failure:** SGLT2 inhibitors are strongly recommended to reduce CKD progression and heart failure hospitalizations.
- **Patients without established ASCVD/CKD:** Choice depends on glycemic efficacy, hypoglycemia risk (prefer DPP-4 inhibitors, GLP-1 RAs, or SGLT2is over sulfonylureas), weight impact, and affordability.

## 4. Acute Complications & Emergency Management

### Hypoglycemia Management (Rule of 15)
Hypoglycemia is clinically defined as blood glucose < 70 mg/dL (3.9 mmol/L).
- **Level 1 (Alert):** 54 - 69 mg/dL.
- **Level 2 (Clinically Significant):** < 54 mg/dL.
- **Level 3 (Severe):** Severe cognitive impairment requiring external assistance regardless of glucose level.
- **Treatment (Rule of 15):** Ingest 15 g of fast-acting carbohydrate (e.g., 4 glucose tablets, 1/2 cup juice or regular soda). Recheck blood glucose after 15 minutes. If still < 70 mg/dL, repeat 15 g of carbohydrate. Once normalized, consume a small snack or regular meal.
- **Severe Hypoglycemia:** Administer intramuscular/subcutaneous glucagon (1 mg) or intranasal glucagon (3 mg) and call emergency services immediately.

### Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA) and Hyperosmolar Hyperglycemic State (HHS)
- **Signs of DKA:** Deep rapid breathing (Kussmaul breathing), fruity/acetone breath odor, nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, altered mental status, blood glucose usually > 250 mg/dL with elevated blood/urine ketones and arterial pH < 7.30.
- **Emergency Protocol:** Immediate hospitalization, aggressive IV isotonic fluid resuscitation, IV regular insulin infusion, and strict potassium monitoring.

## 5. Chronic Complications and Preventive Screening
- **Diabetic Retinopathy:** Annual dilated eye examination by an optometrist or ophthalmologist starting at diagnosis for Type 2 diabetes.
- **Diabetic Nephropathy:** Annual urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio (uACR) and estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR).
- **Diabetic Neuropathy and Foot Care:** Comprehensive annual visual and monofilament foot examination; daily patient self-inspection for ulcers or sores.
- **Cardiovascular Risk Reduction:** Blood pressure target < 130/80 mmHg; moderate-to-high intensity statin therapy for individuals aged 40-75 years.
